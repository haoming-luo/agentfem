The authors organize two inelastic rates by short- and long-time primary
creep, rather than imposing a high-/low-stress interpretation. They fit
strain in stress control and stress in strain control jointly, including
mixed-control experiments. They explicitly describe some individual tests
less well than others. Their plotted agreement is not our quantitative
acceptance certificate, and their model excludes tertiary creep.

## Equation audit before adding code

At constant temperature, their equations have the same structural ingredients
as our existing scalar backbone: two overstress flows, separate backstresses,
Armstrong–Frederick recovery multiplied by a power of normalized backstress,
and static recovery. In our notation, C maps to H, gamma to H/a, and the
normalized-backstress exponent to chi. Tensor normalization requires care;
this is a structural comparison, not a verified tensor implementation or
coefficient conversion. The thermal modulus derivative in their equation (3)
vanishes in the present isothermal reduction.

Therefore adding another module named after this paper would duplicate an
existing equation family. The actionable question is whether the current
identified branches exhibit the necessary temporal separation under actual
mixed-control histories. A branch label alone does not establish that.
Keep both delayed recoverable strains and the original directional rule as
controls. Do not borrow coefficients from a different temperature or heat.

## Existing trajectory discriminator

The history-memory audit bounds the homogeneous contribution of backstress
present at the beginning of the 1800 s hold. It does not bound backstress
regenerated by subsequent inelastic flow. For the archived shared-history
card, the SLC fast B contribution is below 1.6e-88 MPa at the hold end; the
slow B initial contribution is bounded by approximately 0.291 MPa. The
initial B stresses are negative, so preserving them cannot simply be called
an increase in positive resistance. At the following positive-hold entry,
the shared-history static factors are approximately 0.8682 (SLR) and 0.8635
(SLC). This card creates little separation through that scalar history alone.
These are conditional observations, not a proof against every shared-history
parameter set or against flow-driven regeneration.

Direct replay now separates signed backstress generation, dynamic recovery
and static recovery. For the first SLC positive hold, fast B contributions
are +374.740, -153.712 and -231.285 MPa, summing to -10.258 MPa. Thus the
small net change hides substantial competing terms. During the preceding
long creep hold its flow-generated increment is only +0.00214 MPa. The
contribution integrals close against the independently integrated state to
better than 4e-10 MPa in all four replayed holds. This diagnoses a change in
which processes dominate; it does not measure microscopic backstress or prove
that reducing static recovery will improve all three protocols.

For fixed histories p<q and residual fraction rho, the largest absolute
difference between factors rho+(1-rho)*exp(-b*p) and
rho+(1-rho)*exp(-b*q), over b>=0, occurs at
b=log(q/p)/(q-p). Using this card's hold-entry histories and rho gives
0.007514 for shared accumulated strain and 0.015332 for A-only history.
B-only history has maximum absolute contrast 0.014944 but the SLC history
is smaller, reversing the direction of the factor change. The closed form
is checked against scalar optimization and near-equal/zero limits. These
conditional factor bounds are not bounds on a reidentified stress trajectory;
they are a reason not to expect a large distinction from changing only the
exponential history scale. No actual fit bound is changed.
